      How Verity works

      Fact-checkers ask whether advice is true. Verity also asks whether it's true for you, and shows its reasoning every time.

      What it reviews

      • What the advice claims, in one line.
      • How well mainstream expert guidance supports it: dermatology, medicine, public health and basic consumer finance.
      • Red flags, like guaranteed results, urgency or fear.
      • Whether it fits the profile you filled in, and which fields it used.
      • A good place to start, and when to ask a professional.

      What it doesn't do

      • Diagnose a condition or suggest a dose.
      • Recommend a brand, product, stock, coin or fund.
      • Give personal investment, tax or legal advice. It explains general principles.
      • Cite anything outside its source library. Every source it shows was checked by hand, and the AI only picks from that list, so it never makes up a source, a quote or a number.
      • Judge creators. It critiques the claim, never the person.

      Methodology

      Before each release, Verity reviews 20 test claims with known answers, three times over. These are the latest results.

      Where your data lives

      Your profile and My Proof stay in this browser on this device. When you ask for a review, the advice and only the profile fields you filled in are sent to Claude to write the verdict. Verity has no analytics and no tracking.

      Its limits

      Verity uses AI, and AI can be wrong. Evidence changes over time. "The evidence is mixed" is a complete answer. For symptoms, medications, pregnancy, mental health or large debts, talk to a professional.

      If you're going through something hard right now, you can call or text 988, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline in the US.
